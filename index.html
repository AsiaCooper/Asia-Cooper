Asia Amerie

The Beauty in What You Wear.

The Problem

The fashion industry often prioritizes mass production over individuality, leaving consumers—especially young women—feeling disconnected from what they wear. Many struggle to find clothing that fits their body, reflects their identity, and delivers a sense of confidence for special moments. Affordable options sacrifice quality, while high-end custom designs are often inaccessible or lack a personalized experience. This creates a gap in the market for a brand that delivers both luxury customization and emotional connection at a more attainable level.

Our Solution

Asia Amerié provides high-quality, custom-designed fashion pieces tailored to each client’s vision, body, and occasion. The brand focuses on delivering a personalized, luxury experience—from consultation and design to final fitting—ensuring each piece is both visually striking and confidence-enhancing. What sets Asia Amerié apart is its combination of storytelling, body positivity, and craftsmanship, creating not just clothing but an experience. Customers discover the brand through curated digital content, engage through consultations, and receive a one-of-a-kind piece designed specifically for them.

Target Market

Asia Amerié targets women ages 18–30, primarily college students and young professionals, with moderate to high disposable income who value confidence, individuality, and elevated fashion. These consumers are active on social media, invest in appearance for key life moments, and seek unique, statement pieces rather than fast fashion. TAM (Total Addressable Market): U.S. women’s apparel market exceeding $190B SAM (Serviceable Available Market): Custom and occasion-based fashion within Gen Z and Millennial consumers SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market): College campuses and urban markets, starting with the Southeast region.

Revenue Model

Asia Amerié generates revenue through custom garment sales, with pricing based on design complexity, materials, and craftsmanship. Custom gowns range from $400–$1,000+, depending on detail, fabric, and customization level. Custom gowns ($400–$1,000+ premium offering) Consultation fees + deposits to secure bookings Purse & accessory line (future expansion) Limited-edition collections and seasonal drops Future styling packages and brand services Model: Direct-to-consumer with a pre-order and custom booking system to minimize inventory costs and reduce financial risk.

Competitive Advantage

Asia Amerié’s competitive advantage lies in its ability to merge luxury customization with a strong emotional brand identity. Unlike traditional retailers, the brand offers a fully personalized experience centered on confidence and self-expression. Strong brand storytelling and lifestyle marketing Founder-led design with direct customer interaction Focus on body positivity and inclusivity Limited production model creating exclusivity Early brand positioning within college and emerging markets

Capstone Reflection

Your transformation from student to professional, in your own words.

01

My Journey at Benedict

My journey at Benedict College has been a process of both professional and personal transformation. I came in with ambition, but Benedict gave me direction, discipline, and the confidence to lead. As a Business Marketing major, I learned that business is not just about ideas—it’s about execution, consistency, and understanding people. Through courses like BA 432, I gained a deeper understanding of entrepreneurship, especially what it takes to turn a concept into a structured, viable business. I learned how to think critically about pricing, target markets, operations, and long-term sustainability. BA 432 challenged me to move beyond surface-level thinking and truly develop my brand, Asia Amerié, as a real business. It forced me to analyze my decisions, back them with data, and refine my strategy. More importantly, I learned about myself—how I handle pressure, how I lead, and how I push through uncertainty. Senior Defense Day was a defining moment for me. Standing in front of a panel and presenting my business validated my growth. It strengthened my confidence in my knowledge, my vision, and my ability to communicate effectively in high-stakes environments. It showed me that I am capable of not only building a business but also defending it with clarity and confidence.

02

Challenges & Breakthroughs

One of the biggest challenges I faced while developing my business concept was refining my value proposition and pricing strategy. Initially, I struggled with balancing quality and cost, especially knowing that my brand, Asia Amerié, is centered around high-quality, custom fashion rather than affordability. I had to shift my mindset from trying to appeal to everyone to focusing on a specific target market that values exclusivity and personalization. This required deeper research, honest self-reflection, and the willingness to reposition my brand. Another challenge was time management and maintaining consistency while balancing multiple responsibilities, including academics, leadership roles, and internships. There were moments where I felt overwhelmed, especially when deadlines for the capstone approached. However, I overcame this by becoming more structured, planning ahead, and holding myself accountable. I also learned the importance of asking for feedback and actually applying it. The biggest breakthroughs came from moments of failure or discomfort. There were times when my ideas were challenged, and I had to go back and rework entire sections of my business plan. Those moments taught me resilience and adaptability. If I could do something differently, I would start refining my concept earlier and seek feedback sooner instead of waiting until later stages. Overall, these challenges strengthened my ability to think strategically and operate like a true business owner.

03

My Professional Future

In five years, I see myself established in corporate America, working in a marketing or business strategy role where I can contribute to brand growth, customer engagement, and revenue generation. At the same time, I plan to continue growing my business, Asia Amerié, into a recognized brand known for high-quality, custom fashion and exceptional customer experience. My long-term goal is to successfully operate in both spaces—corporate and entrepreneurship—using each to strengthen the other. My capstone experience directly connects to these goals because it gave me a realistic understanding of what it takes to build and sustain a business. It wasn’t just about creating a plan—it was about thinking like a strategist, making data-driven decisions, and preparing for real-world challenges. The skills I developed in BA 432, such as strategic planning, financial analysis, problem-solving, and professional communication, will be essential as I move forward in my career. I also carry forward the confidence and leadership skills I developed at Benedict College. I want to be the kind of leader who is strategic, decisive, and forward-thinking, but also adaptable and people-focused. I aim to lead with both vision and execution—someone who not only sets goals but ensures they are achieved. Ultimately, I want to create impact, build meaningful brands, and open doors for others while continuing to grow into the best version of myself professionally and personally.
